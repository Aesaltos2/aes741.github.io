Week 4 — Task 3 · Ethical Dilemma

Alternative Ethical Approach

Misuse of Administrative Privileges — A Better Path Forward

Essential Ethical Principles

Honesty Accountability Fairness Respect for Persons Responsibility Transparency

The administrator's actions violated all six principles simultaneously. He was not honest about how he used his access. He avoided accountability for 18 months. He was not fair to colleagues whose private data he accessed without consent. He showed no respect for the persons whose information he treated as available to his curiosity. He abdicated professional responsibility and operated in deliberate opacity.

The Alternative Approach

  • Stop at the boundary. Recognize that accessing employee PII has no legitimate business justification for his role and choose not to proceed — not because monitoring would catch him, but because it is wrong.
  • Report the capability gap. Instead of exploiting the absence of PAM controls, surface that gap to management: "My credentials give me access beyond my assigned scope. This is a security risk. Here is my recommendation."
  • Advocate for ethical culture. Use professional standing that comes with elevated access to push for stronger accountability mechanisms — because it protects the organization and the people it serves.

Why This Alternative Is Better — Outcomes for Everyone

What Actually Happened Hundreds of employees had PII exposed. The administrator faced criminal charges. The organization lost trust, faced regulatory scrutiny, and spent significant resources on forensic investigation. The 18-month gap revealed a culture that normalized unchecked privileged access.
With the Ethical Alternative Employee dignity and privacy are protected. The administrator builds a reputation as a trusted professional. The organization gains a more secure architecture. A culture of proactive accountability replaces reactive damage control. The administrator's career is built on a foundation of trust that sustains long-term growth.

The alternative approach is not harder — it is simply a choice to treat integrity as a professional standard rather than an optional virtue. The administrator who reports the access gap is not sacrificing anything real. He is investing in the kind of professional reputation and organizational culture that creates durable, meaningful work.

Week 4 — Task 4 · IT Project

Project Refinement Report

The project selected for refinement is the Full Office Deployment Proposal originally developed in IT 235 — a complete Windows Server 2022 Datacenter deployment for PromisedLand.com, a hybrid call center and tourist guide operation with 45 employees in Laie, Hawaii.

Returning to this project through IT 497 revealed that technical competence alone is insufficient. The original proposal documented what to install but did not ask who the system serves or what the human consequences of failures would be. Click each refinement to expand the before/after detail.

01RDP Security — MFA, VPN Tunnel & Lockout Policy Security﹢
Before

RDP on default port 3389 with no MFA, IP restrictions, or lockout policy.

After

Non-standard port (33899), NLA, MFA via Microsoft Authenticator, VPN tunnel requirement, 5-attempt lockout policy.

Port 3389 is one of the most actively scanned ports on the internet. Automated brute-force tools continuously probe it — this configuration was a front door for ransomware. The refinement closes that door without impeding legitimate remote access.

02Tiered RTO/RPO Recovery Matrix Infrastructure﹢
Before

Single undefined RTO; RPO 60 sec via StorageCraft. No tier differentiation.

After

Tier 1 (Call Center): RTO 15 min/RPO 5 min. Tier 2 (Admin): 2hr/30 min. Tier 3 (Archive): 24hr/24hr. Hot standby via ShadowXafe.

For a 45-person call center, every minute of downtime has direct revenue impact. The tiered framework aligns recovery investment with business criticality and provides honest, measurable commitments.

03AD — 4-Tier RBAC, LAPS & JIT Access Access Control﹢
Before

Principle of Least Privilege stated conceptually. No GPO, RBAC structure, or PAM rules documented.

After

4-tier AD roles. Microsoft LAPS for local admin password rotation. JIT access for all elevated ops. SIEM logging of all privileged actions.

Directly informed by the Week 2 ethical dilemma: an administrator who abused access for 18 months because there were no PAM controls. Stating PoLP without implementing it is documentation theater. This refinement makes the commitment real.

04IIS Certificate Lifecycle Management Security﹢
Before

Certificate installed on IIS. No CA hierarchy, renewal workflow, or expiry monitoring.

After

AD CS root CA + intermediate issuing CA. Auto-renewal via GPO. Let's Encrypt + win-acme for public services. 60-day expiry alerts.

An expired certificate causes immediate service outages and security warnings. In a call center dependent on web-based tools, certificate expiry causes real operational disruption. Automation removes the human dependency that causes most expiry incidents.

054-VLAN Network Segmentation Infrastructure﹢
Before

Segmentation mentioned but no VLAN architecture, routing rules, or traffic isolation defined.

After

VLAN 10 (Mgmt), 20 (Call Center), 30 (Tourist – public-facing), 40 (Guest Wi-Fi – isolated). Tourist stations have no route to VLANs 10 or 20.

Without segmentation, a compromised tourist guide station — public-facing — could provide lateral movement access to call center and admin systems. Isolating public-facing systems is the single most impactful network security improvement at this scale.

06Employee Data Privacy Policy & Consent Ethics﹢
Before

No data privacy policy, consent process, classification framework, or retention schedule.

After

Data Classification Policy (4 tiers). Personnel = Restricted. Written employee notices. Formal retention schedule with documented exceptions.

Employees deserve to know what data is collected about them and who can access it. This is not a compliance checkbox — it is respect for persons. The classification policy also makes access decisions clearer, creating friction that makes casual overreach less likely.

07Biometric Consent & Alternative Access Ethics﹢
Before

Mantrap with facial recognition documented. No consent, storage policy, or opt-out provisions.

After

Written informed consent required. Biometric data in encrypted isolated database. Hardware key alternative available — no penalty for declining.

Requiring consent and providing a penalty-free alternative ensures security does not create a coercive employment condition. Security should protect people, not require them to surrender rights as a condition of employment.

08WCAG 2.1 AA Accessibility for Tourist Stations Ethics﹢
Before

Tourist guide interfaces not addressed for accessibility or multilingual support.

After

WCAG 2.1 Level AA compliance required. Screen reader, keyboard nav, color contrast, multilingual (English/Japanese/Spanish). Included in deployment acceptance criteria.

Tourist stations serve the general public in Hawaii — a linguistically and demographically diverse context. A public-facing system inaccessible to visitors with disabilities fails its stated purpose. Accessibility is a condition of genuine public service, not an add-on.

Spiritual & Professional Integration

Reflective Experience

This week's reflective activity was not a simulation — it was a lived experience. I attended the Bountiful Utah Temple, where my wife and I serve together as temple workers each week. We also visited the Salt Lake Temple Visitors' Center, where a detailed representation of the temple's interior rooms invites reflection on the architecture and purpose of those sacred spaces.

Serving as a temple worker is a different experience from attending as a patron. As a worker, your presence directly affects the experience of others. You are not there for yourself first. That responsibility sharpens the mind and softens the heart simultaneously — and it has taught me that consecration is not a dramatic, once-in-a-lifetime event. It is a quiet, weekly, unrecognized act of showing up.

At the Visitors' Center, I was struck by something I had not articulated before: the temple is designed to teach through environment. Every room, every symbol, every progression communicates something about the relationship between God and His children. That realization landed differently for me as someone who builds digital systems — every user interface I design also communicates something, consciously or not, about the values of the person who built it.

Elder Bednar's analogy of intertwined rope strands came to mind repeatedly (Bednar, 2018). The strands of doctrine, principle, and practice are strongest when woven together. I have spent three weeks in this course trying to articulate how my spiritual life and professional life are connected. This week, I did not have to try. President Nelson (2021) promised that making time for the Lord would not diminish our capacity but expand it. I have found that promise to be true — the temple is where I remember who I am, whose I am, and what the work is ultimately for.

As I build toward a career in AI and Automation Engineering, I carry from this experience a renewed conviction: the most important thing I will bring to any technical role is not my knowledge of machine learning frameworks or database design. It is the character built covenant by covenant, service session by service session — shaped by diligence, integrity, and consecrated service. That character is the foundation on which everything else in this portfolio rests.
