Professional Ethics

Ethical Reflections

Case Study: Misuse of Administrative Privileges in System Administration

The Dilemma

A senior systems administrator at a financial services company abused elevated access privileges over 18 months — accessing confidential employee records, PII, and salary data without legitimate business need, and installing unauthorized monitoring software on executive workstations (Verizon, 2023). The breach was discovered during a routine audit, not active monitoring, revealing a systemic organizational failure.

Parties Involved

  • The System Administrator — trusted employee with elevated access who betrayed that trust
  • Executive Leadership & HR — individuals whose workstations and records were accessed without consent
  • Hundreds of affected employees — whose PII and salary data were exposed
  • The IT Security Team — failed to detect activity for 18 months due to absent PAM controls
  • Regulatory Bodies — potential CCPA and financial industry compliance obligations triggered

Initial Response

  • Immediate revocation of credentials and termination of employment
  • Forensic investigation by internal team and third-party cybersecurity firm
  • Notification to affected employees regarding unauthorized data access
  • Overhaul of privileged access management (PAM) policies; implementation of least-privilege and just-in-time access controls
  • Legal review for liability under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)

Analysis & Personal Commitment

This case illustrates a core truth: technical access controls are never a substitute for ethical formation. The administrator had the access, the time, and the organizational cover to act as he did. What he lacked was integrity. As someone who has held elevated system access for over eight years, I am committed to being the professional whose character is the first line of defense — not the last. This analysis is a public declaration of that commitment.

Elder Bednar's analogy of intertwined rope strands came to mind repeatedly (Bednar, 2018). The strands of doctrine, principle, and practice are strongest when woven together. I have spent three weeks in this course trying to articulate how my spiritual life and professional life are connected. This week, I did not have to try. As I build toward a career in AI and Automation Engineering, I carry from this experience a renewed conviction: the most important thing I will bring to any technical role is not my knowledge of machine learning frameworks or database design.
