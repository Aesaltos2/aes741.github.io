Ongoing Development Action Plan

Casey Hurley's devotional on climbing your own Mount Sinai reframes professional goals as spiritual ascents — each requiring preparation, faith, and the willingness to take the next step even without a full view of the summit. The following action plan is not a to-do list. It is a covenant document: specific, measurable commitments rooted in the three guiding principles that have anchored this entire portfolio.

01
Spiritual · Principle 1

Daily Scripture Study & Reflection Journal

Protect 30 minutes each morning for scripture study and written reflection — before opening any professional task. Connect each day's reading to a current technical or professional challenge. Over six months, this practice will produce a personal theology of work grounded in the gospel.

Measurable indicator: 180 consecutive days of written reflection entries by December 2026.

⏱ Start: Immediately · Review: Monthly
02
Professional · Principle 1 — Diligence

AI & Machine Learning Foundations Certificate

Complete the Google Machine Learning Crash Course and DeepLearning.AI TensorFlow Developer Certificate within 12 months of graduation. These are the foundational credentials for the AI and Automation Engineering career path identified throughout this portfolio. Diligence applied to the frontier.

Measurable indicator: Both certificates earned by June 2027.

⏱ Start: Post-graduation · Duration: 12 months
03
Spiritual · Principle 3

Continued Temple Service — Covenant Renewal

Maintain weekly temple service with my wife at the Bountiful Utah Temple. Treat each session as a professional development activity — not metaphorically, but practically. The character built in consecrated service is the foundation on which every technical credential rests. Renewal of the covenant of consecration is non-negotiable.

Measurable indicator: Uninterrupted weekly service, tracked by personal journal.

⏱ Ongoing · Renewed each week
04
Professional · Principle 2 — Integrity

CompTIA Security+ Certification

Complete CompTIA Security+ within six months of graduation. This credential formalizes the security knowledge demonstrated in the PromisedLand.com refinement project and the ethical dilemma analysis. It also signals to employers a professional commitment to the integrity principle: security is not an add-on, it is architecture.

Measurable indicator: Exam passed by December 2026.

⏱ Start: Post-graduation · Duration: 6 months
05
Professional · Leadership

IT Leadership Role — Target Within 24 Months

Pursue a team lead or systems architect role within 24 months of graduation. The leadership principles developed across this course — councils, collaborative problem-solving, ethical culture advocacy — are leadership skills, not just spiritual ones. The goal is a role where those skills shape the professional environment of a team, not just my individual practice.

Measurable indicator: Leadership role by June 2028, with documented team impact metrics.

⏱ Start: Post-graduation · Target: 24 months

Community Technology Service — Rose Park / Salt Lake City

Within 12 months of graduation, develop and deliver a free digital literacy or cybersecurity workshop for immigrant and refugee families in the Rose Park area of Salt Lake City — a community I know and that shaped my understanding of what technology access means for vulnerable populations. This is Mosiah 2:17 made operational: using IT skills in consecrated service to the community that received me.

Measurable indicator: Workshop delivered to minimum 20 participants by June 2027.

Week 6 — Final Submission · Reflective Essay

Portfolio Alignment & Evolution

When I began this portfolio in Week 3, I had a clear sense of the professional I had been — eight years of trusted technical work, a completed engineering degree, a full-stack development certificate, and a growing conviction that my next frontier was AI and Automation Engineering. What I did not have was a coherent articulation of why I had made the choices I had, or how the spiritual and professional dimensions of my life were actually connected rather than merely parallel.

"The most important thing I will contribute to any technical role is not skill — it is character. Skills can be learned. Character is built covenant by covenant, choice by choice."

The initial goal for this portfolio was, frankly, modest: document my professional competencies and spiritual principles well enough to present them coherently. What happened instead was something closer to what Casey Hurley described in his devotional — climbing the mountain changes you. Not the view from the top. The climb itself.

What has changed from my initial goals: I began with three principles that I understood conceptually. I finish with three principles I can trace through specific decisions, specific projects, and specific unobserved moments. Diligence is no longer an abstraction — it is the choice I made to reenroll after immigration disrupted my studies, the choice to pursue FUNDET alongside full-time work, the choice to revisit the PromisedLand.com deployment not because I was required to, but because I could see how it should be better. Integrity is no longer a virtue I claim — it is a technical architecture I can specify and implement: JIT access, behavioral monitoring, PAM controls, biometric consent. Service is no longer a sentiment — it is a community workshop in Rose Park, a specific address, a measurable commitment.

The evolution that surprised me most was in how I understand the ethical dilemma case. When I first analyzed the administrator's privilege abuse in Week 2, I understood it as a case about an individual who made wrong choices. By Week 5, I understood it as a case about systemic conditions that made wrong choices easier than right ones — and about my personal responsibility not just to make right choices myself, but to build the organizational environments where right choices are the path of least resistance. That shift from individual to systemic ethical thinking is, I believe, the difference between a technically competent professional and a leader.

Elder Morrison's devotional on unexpected paths also gave me language for something I had experienced but not named: the most significant growth in my professional journey came from paths I did not choose. Immigration was not a plan — it was an unexpected path that built resilience, cross-cultural communication, and a bilingual capacity that has become one of my most valuable professional assets. Moving to Salt Lake City was not on a five-year plan — it was an act of faith that led to Ensign College and to this portfolio. The unexpected paths taught me to hold plans lightly and convictions firmly.

Alignment with initial goals: The portfolio exceeds what I initially imagined it could be — not in length, but in coherence. The spiritual and professional sections do not sit alongside each other as separate chapters. They are intertwined in the way Elder Bednar described: each strand made stronger by the others. The PromisedLand.com refinement report is a technical document that is also an ethical manifesto. The ethical dilemma analysis is a security architecture recommendation that is also a personal covenant. The action plan is a professional roadmap that is also a spiritual commitment. That integration was the goal I did not know I had when the course began.

Future use of this portfolio: This portfolio will accompany every job application I submit. Not because it demonstrates technical competency — though it does — but because it demonstrates character in professional context. The employer who reviews this portfolio will know what I value, how I reason about ethical dilemmas, how I approach continuous learning, and what I am building toward. I intend to continue updating it as I complete the Security+ certification, the AI/ML coursework, and the community workshop — so that the portfolio remains a living document of ongoing consecration rather than a snapshot of a course I finished.

Casey Hurley said that climbing your own Mount Sinai means committing to the ascent before you can see the summit. I began this course with a clearer view of where I had been than where I was going. I finish it with a specific, measurable, covenant-grounded vision of where I am going — and with the conviction that the diligence, integrity, and consecrated service that carried me to this point will carry me the rest of the way.
